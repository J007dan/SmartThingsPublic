Every news app uses motion to make you feel that something is happening right now. This one is arguing the opposite. So the question is not whether motion is nice, it is whether a given movement says the right thing.

The rule: motion here should demonstrate waiting, not reward attention. It runs once, it finishes, and it never loops. Anything that pulses, throbs, or refuses to settle is telling the reader to stay alert — which is the exact reflex this app exists to lower.

Worth it — once per session

Fourteen ticks count in, left to right, and the crimson one lands last. It teaches the entire premise of the app before a word is read, and it takes about 1.3 seconds — a pause short enough to tolerate and long enough to feel deliberate.

Never ship this

Identical artwork, wrong intent. Bouncing ticks, a throbbing accent, a drifting wordmark, blinking labels — nothing here is unusual for a news app. Watch it for ten seconds and notice that it will not let you settle. That restlessness is the product promise being contradicted by its own splash screen.
