And then …? — three routes

Three identity directions for a retrospective news app. Each carries a mark, a wordmark lockup, and small-size proofs. All three use the same palette: newsprint #F4F4F2, editorial charcoal #1A1D20, slate #5A6578, and a single restrained crimson #B9381E reserved for punctuation and the accent tick.

Two production notes

The ellipsis in every lockup is drawn as vector circles rather than typed periods, so its rhythm and the gap before the question mark are set optically instead of inherited from font metrics. Each lockup was measured in a browser and centred to within a tenth of a pixel.

The SVGs pull Playfair Display and Inter from Google Fonts and fall back to Georgia and Helvetica. Webfonts load when the file is opened directly or inlined in HTML, but are blocked when it is used as an <img> source. For Figma, print, or app-store assets, convert the text to outlines first.

01

Classic Editorial

The ellipsis set between a newspaper double rule — the section divider every reader recognises without being able to name.

Mark
Three dots between editorial rules. The third turns crimson: the answer arrives at the end of the wait.
Type
Playfair Display carries the wordmark; Inter handles the kicker, tracked wide at 3.4.
Strength
Instantly legible as journalism, and the simplest of the three to reproduce in one colour or at 16px.
Risk
The most familiar of the three. Reads as authoritative, but less ownable — the rules-and-dots device is common in editorial design.

On dark, the ink inverts and the accent must lift from #B9381E to about #E0563A — the brief's crimson is too dark to hold against a near-black ground.

02

Modern Minimalist & Timeline

Fourteen ticks. Day 0 in charcoal, day 14 in crimson, the twelve days between them receding into grey.

Mark
The delay is literally counted, not alluded to. Anyone who looks twice can count the gap.
Type
Inter SemiBold at −1.6 tracking, with one deliberate inversion: the question mark is the serif, the single editorial note in a geometric lockup.
Strength
The only mark of the three that encodes the actual product rule. Reduces cleanly to three ticks for a favicon.
Risk
At very small sizes the fourteen ticks blur into a rule, which is why the reduced three-tick variant is part of the system rather than an afterthought.

On dark, the ink inverts and the accent must lift from #B9381E to about #E0563A — the brief's crimson is too dark to hold against a near-black ground.

03

Typographic Emphasis

A question mark built entirely from an ellipsis — the dots swell and darken as they curve, resolving into a crimson point.

Mark
Punctuation telling the story exactly as briefed: the trailing-off and the answer are the same glyph.
Type
Playfair Display Italic gives the wordmark a reflective, essayistic voice; Inter holds the micro-label.
Strength
The most ownable and the most distinctive. It is a mark nobody else has.
Risk
The most fragile small. It holds to about 28px and below that the dots merge — it needs a solid-drawn variant for favicon use.

On dark, the ink inverts and the accent must lift from #B9381E to about #E0563A — the brief's crimson is too dark to hold against a near-black ground.

Which one

Route 02 for the product, route 03 for the brand. They are not really competing — they do different jobs, and the strongest outcome is probably to use both.

02 is the only mark that encodes the actual rule of the app. Fourteen ticks is a fact about the product, not a mood about it, and it reduces to three ticks at favicon size without losing meaning. That makes it the safer app icon and the better system.

03 is the mark nobody else could own. A question mark assembled from an ellipsis is exactly the brief's "punctuation tells the story," and it is the one people would remember. It is also the most fragile below 28px, so it wants a solid-drawn variant before it goes on a home screen.

01 is the safest and the least distinctive. Worth keeping as the editorial-context lockup — a masthead rule for a newsletter or a print footer — rather than the primary identity.

One thing to decide deliberately: this palette is warm newsprint, and the app as built runs on a cool twilight blue-grey. They are different worlds. Adopting one of these means repainting the app, or consciously letting the brand and the reading surface diverge.
