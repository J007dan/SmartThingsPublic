    Notice which bars are long.

    Most of what follows dominated a news cycle and moved almost nothing. A few items barely registered and are permanent. That inversion is the reason this app exists — the volume of a story on the day it breaks tells you very little about whether it will matter.

    Switch lenses on any card. The account of who was furious changes completely. The outcome never does.

    How a story gets resolved

    Every card is classified into one of twelve recurring shapes — vows to sue, ruled then stayed, referred not charged, anchor then soften, and so on. The shape is shown at the bottom of each outcome, with the specific docket to check and how long that shape usually takes.

    This is what makes the app affordable to run. Classification turns "what came of it?" from open-ended research into a named source checked on a known clock — and where a shape has run to completion before, its own history becomes a dated, falsifiable prediction about the next one.

    Editorial checklist — run every session
    • Pull the Supreme Court's order and opinion lists; trace every decision back to the furor it settles, however old.
    • Re-check open tariff anchors for the quiet walk-back, and record whether the anchor held.
    • Check the FCC Covered List and conditional-approval announcements for robotics movement.
    • Score every live prediction. A missed call is published, not deleted.
    • Confirm both lenses are populated. A card with one empty lens is a finding, not a gap to hide.
